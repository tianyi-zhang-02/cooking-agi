# Linear algebra: projection, covariance, and least squares

[中文](linear-algebra.md) · **English** · [Review map](../README.en.md)

> Reading time: ~11 min · Prerequisites: matrix multiplication and inner products · Last reviewed: 2026-10

Go beyond calculating eigenvalues. Which directions are independent? Where does a fit project the data? When is a solution unstable?

## 1 · Rank–nullity: where input directions go

For $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$, the column space contains possible outputs; the null space contains inputs mapped to zero.

$$
\operatorname{rank}(A)+\dim\ker A=n.
$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Proof: extend a basis of the null space</summary>

Take a null-space basis $v_1,\dots,v_k$ and extend it to an input-space basis $v_1,\dots,v_n$. The images of the last n−k vectors span the column space because the first k images vanish. Those images are independent: otherwise a nonzero combination of the last vectors would lie in the null space, contradicting independence of the full basis. Thus rank=n−k.

</details>

Ax=b is solvable exactly when b belongs to the column space. Uniqueness additionally requires a trivial null space. For a square matrix, invertibility, full rank, and nonzero determinant are equivalent.

## 2 · Projection: where the residual must point

Project y onto the line generated by nonzero u:

$$
\hat y=\frac{u^\top y}{u^\top u}u.
$$

Minimize $\|y-cu\|^2$ over c. Differentiation gives $u^\top(y-cu)=0$: the residual is orthogonal to the available direction.

For design matrix X, minimizing $\|y-X\beta\|^2$ yields the normal equations:

$$
X^\top X\hat\beta=X^\top y.
$$

Full column rank gives $(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y$. With deficient rank, fitted values remain unique but coefficients need not be. The pseudoinverse selects a minimum-norm solution.

## 3 · A regression example you can compute by hand

Fit y≈a+bx to (0,1), (1,2), (2,2). Here $\bar x=1,\bar y=5/3$, slope $\sum(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)/\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2=1/2$, and intercept 7/6.

Residuals are −1/6,1/3,−1/6. Their sum and inner product with x both vanish, checking orthogonality to the design columns.

**Do not usually compute the inverse explicitly.** QR or SVD is typically more stable. Forming normal equations also squares the two-norm condition number.

## 4 · Why covariance matrices are positive semidefinite

For a random vector Z with finite second moments, $\Sigma=E[(Z-\mu)(Z-\mu)^\top]$. For any a:

$$
a^\top\Sigma a=\operatorname{Var}(a^\top Z)\ge0.
$$

This is positive semidefiniteness (PSD). Having every correlation entry between −1 and 1 is not enough to make a valid correlation matrix.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Counterexample: three pairwise correlations all equal −0.9</summary>

The 3×3 matrix with diagonal 1 and off-diagonal −0.9 has eigenvalue 1−1.8=−0.8 along the all-ones vector. It is not PSD. Pairwise correlations cannot be assembled arbitrarily.

</details>

## 5 · Eigendecomposition and SVD

A real symmetric matrix has orthogonal eigendecomposition $A=Q\Lambda Q^\top$. It is PSD exactly when all eigenvalues are nonnegative. We invoke the spectral theorem here rather than prove it fully.

Every real matrix has singular value decomposition $A=U\Sigma V^\top$. Right singular vectors are input directions, singular values are stretch factors, and left singular vectors are output directions. Rank counts nonzero singular values.

| Question | Useful representation |
| --- | --- |
| Principal directions of covariance | Symmetric eigendecomposition / PCA |
| Rank deficiency or ill-conditioning | SVD, pseudoinverse |
| Full-rank least squares | QR |
| Positive-definite solves and correlated Gaussian sampling | Cholesky |

For positive-definite Σ, Cholesky gives Σ=LLᵀ. If ε~N(0,I), then Lε has covariance Σ. A singular PSD matrix can instead be handled through eigendecomposition; ordinary positive-definite Cholesky is not guaranteed to work.

## 6 · PCA and Ridge: trust unstable directions less

Variance along unit v is $v^\top\Sigma v$. In an eigenbasis this is a nonnegative weighted average of eigenvalues. Its maximum is the largest eigenvalue, attained by a corresponding eigenvector.

Ridge minimizes $\|y-X\beta\|^2+\lambda\|\beta\|^2$, λ>0, giving:

$$
\hat\beta_\lambda=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y.
$$

It trades bias for stability; it is not universally superior. Intercepts are usually handled separately without penalization, and feature scaling changes the penalty's meaning.

## 7 · Check the assumptions again

Zero covariance need not mean independence. Invertibility does not mean numerical stability. Low training error does not establish predictive quality. Determinant measures volume scaling and trace sums eigenvalues; neither replaces the full spectrum.
