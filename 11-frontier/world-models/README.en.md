# World models: what the term means now

[中文](README.md) · **English**

> Reading time: ~7 min · Last reviewed: 2026-10

“World model” appears in reinforcement learning, interactive video, and robotics, but it does not always mean the same kind of model. One predicts the state after an action; another generates the next frames; another predicts only the features needed for a task.

These methods often aim to reduce the need for real-world trials. An agent can practice inside a model, a planner can compare actions, and a test system can generate scenarios. We organize the series by what each model predicts and how that prediction is used, then examine its capabilities and limits.

## Why the term is so confusing

Primary sources define it differently. For [Ha and Schmidhuber (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122) it is a generative model of a reinforcement-learning environment, which lets them "train our agent entirely inside of its own hallucinated dream generated by its world model …" (abstract) and then transfer the policy back; [LeCun (2022)](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf) makes it one module of an autonomous agent that predicts future states in an abstract representation space (§3); [NVIDIA Cosmos (2025-01)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03575) frames it as a "digital twin" for physical AI; [World Labs (2026-06)](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/taxonomy-of-world-models) sorts world models by output into renderers (observations), simulators (state such as geometry and physics) and planners (actions). [A July 2026 perspective paper by Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06401) says plainly in its abstract that there is no consensus on what a world model is.

The term has also become a label for companies and deals: AMI Labs announced a $1.03B round on 2026-03-10 ([AMI Labs](https://amilabs.xyz/updates)), and AMD announced on 2026-09-28 that it would acquire World Labs for about $8.2B in stock ([AMD press release](https://newsroom.amd.com/news/amd-acquire-world-labs/)). When you see it, do not assume which kind is meant.

## Ask 4 questions first

| Question | Common answers |
| --- | --- |
| Predicts what? | The next latent state and reward; the next few video frames; the embedding of a future clip; a 3D scene; or only reward, value and policy |
| In what space? | A compact latent vector; pixels, or video tokens / latents; a learned embedding; explicit 3D (Gaussian splats, meshes) |
| Conditioned on what, and on actions? | Robot actions, keyboard and mouse, ego trajectory, text events; some only control the camera, some take no action at all |
| Used for what? | Planning; training a policy in imagination; simulating and evaluating other policies; generating training data; entertainment |

### One question, several kinds of answer

The whole series uses one teaching example: **a robot arm is about to push a cup toward the edge of a table. Will the cup fall if it pushes 2 cm, or 5 cm?** The numbers are a teaching setup, not any paper's experiment. Different world models answer in completely different forms:

| Kind | What it returns for "push 5 cm" | Who uses the answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dreamer-style latent model | The next latent state, a predicted reward, and whether the episode continues (it ends if the cup falls) | A policy trained in imagination |
| MuZero-style model | Only reward, value and policy; it need not say where the cup is | Tree search |
| Video world model | The next few frames: the cup slides, maybe tips over | A person watching, a scoring evaluator, a policy using it as training data |
| JEPA | The embedding of the future clip, then its distance to the embedding of a "goal image" | A planner such as CEM |
| Interactive video world | A person presses keys to push the cup and sees the scene respond in real time | A player, or an agent under test |

## 6 meanings

Common usage falls into 6 kinds; each row lists 1–2 representatives:

| Meaning | Predicts | Space | Action-conditioned? | Main use | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (a) RL world model | Next latent state, reward, continuation; MuZero predicts only reward, value and policy | Compact latent | Yes | Policy learning in imagination; planning (MCTS, MPC) | [DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104), [MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265) |
| (b) JEPA / latent predictive | Representations of masked or future parts | Learned embedding; no pixel decoder needed | Not in pretraining; yes in the action-conditioned variant | Representation learning; planning by energy minimisation | [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243), [V-JEPA 2-AC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985) |
| (c) Interactive generative video | Next frame or frames | Pixels (via latent tokens or latents) | Yes (keyboard, mouse, camera, text events) | Playable worlds, agent training environments, entertainment | [Genie](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15391), [Genie 3](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/) |
| (d) World foundation models for robots and driving | Future multi-camera video (some add lidar or radar), sometimes actions | Pixels or latents | Yes (ego trajectory, robot actions, text) | Synthetic data, policy evaluation, closed-loop simulation; recently the policy itself | [Cosmos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03575), [GAIA-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20523) |
| (e) 3D / spatial | An explicit 3D scene, or view-consistent frames | 3D or posed frames | Mostly camera only | Content creation, simulation assets, real-to-sim | [Marble](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/marble-world-model), [HY-World 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14268) |
| (f) Implicit world models inside general models | Whether an LLM or video generator internally represents world state | Hidden activations, or generated outputs | n/a | Interpretability; "world simulator" claims | [Othello-GPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382), [Sora tech report](https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/) |

The easiest confusion is between (c) and ordinary video generation. Sora and Veo are prompt-in, clip-out generators; interactive world models also take actions, generate frame by frame in real time and keep the scene consistent.

## A timeline

- **2018-03** [World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122): VAE plus MDN-RNN plus a linear controller; the controller is trained inside the model's "dream" and then transferred.
- **2019-11** [MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265) (DeepMind): the model predicts only reward, value and policy, combined with MCTS.
- **2019-12** [Dreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01603): actor-critic learned on imagined latent trajectories; followed by DreamerV2 (2020-10) and DreamerV3 (2023-01).
- **2022-06** [LeCun's position paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf) proposes JEPA and planning by energy minimisation; [I-JEPA (2023-01)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243) first applies it to images.
- **2024-02** [Genie](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15391) (Google DeepMind): learns latent actions from platformer videos without action labels, yielding an interactive environment.
- **2025-01** [NVIDIA Cosmos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03575): open world foundation models, tokenizers and a data curation pipeline.
- **2025-06** [V-JEPA 2 / V-JEPA 2-AC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985) (Meta FAIR): pretraining on more than one million hours of video, then action conditioning, planning zero-shot on Franka arms.
- **2025-08** [Genie 3](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/): Google DeepMind's blog describes real-time 720p, 24 fps worlds that stay consistent for minutes.
- **2025-09** [Dreamer 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24527): trained only on offline data inside a 2B-parameter video world model, it obtains diamonds in Minecraft.
- **2026-01 to 03** [Project Genie](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/project-genie/) opens to paying subscribers; the [Waymo World Model](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/02/the-waymo-world-model-a-new-frontier-for-autonomous-driving-simulation) post-trains Genie 3 into a driving simulator; [DreamZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15922) generates video and actions together and serves directly as a robot policy; AMI Labs launches, and [LeWorldModel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19312) and [V-JEPA 2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14482) appear.

## As of October 2026

- **Playable video worlds went public.** On 2026-01-29 Google opened Project Genie to US Google AI Ultra subscribers, itself stating that generations last only 60 seconds and may not follow prompts or physics; Runway released a [GWM Worlds 2](https://runway.com/research/introducing-gwm-worlds-2) research preview on 2026-09-03; open models include Skywork's [Matrix-Game 3.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08995) (2026-03) and Overworld's [Waypoint](https://huggingface.co/blog/waypoint-1) (from 2026-01). Most of these models' frame rates and resolutions are self-reported.
- **In robotics and driving, world models moved from making data to evaluating policies to serving as the policy**, as in DreamZero from NVIDIA and collaborators in February 2026. Waymo (2026-02) published no fidelity metrics; Wayve's [GAIA-4](https://wayve.ai/thinking/gaia-4/) (2026-08) gave a single number, "2.5× better reconstruction faithfulness".
- **The JEPA line has new papers; the new company has no model yet.** V-JEPA 2.1 (Meta FAIR, 2026-03-15), LeWorldModel (2026-03-13) and [H-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06805) (2026-10-05) were released; as of 2026-10-15, AMI Labs' updates page lists no model, paper or code.
- **Evaluation shifted from "does it look right" to "is it useful".** Shang et al.'s [WorldArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08971) (2026-02) reports a gap between visual quality and usefulness on embodied tasks; Lu et al.'s [WRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20545) (2026-06, 23 models) finds that models returning to a scene tend to resume it as it was instead of advancing the event that was in motion.

## What has settled and what is still moving

| Fairly settled | Still moving | Signals to watch next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Training a policy inside a learned latent model works on standard tasks: DreamerV3 ran 150+ tasks with one configuration (authors' report, Nature 2025) | How far large video models and offline-only data can go: Dreamer 4's diamond rate is 0.7% (Google DeepMind, 2025-09) | Against the 0.7% baseline as of 2026-10: offline imagination training at higher success rates, or on tasks beyond Minecraft |
| Predicting in embedding space can support zero-shot planning on lab robot arms: V-JEPA 2-AC (Meta's tests in its own two labs, 2025-06) | Collapse-prevention recipes: EMA plus stop-gradient, SIGReg (2025-11), LeWM (2026-03); hierarchical planning with H-JEPA (2026-10) | As of 2026-10-15: whether AMI Labs releases a model; whether JEPA planning leaves lab arms and drops sub-goal images |
| Real-time interactive video worlds can be built: Genie 3 at 720p, 24 fps (DeepMind blog, 2025-08) | Memory and consistency beyond minutes: about 1 minute of visual memory for Genie 3 (DeepMind blog, 2025-08); several 2026 memory benchmarks all report systematic failures | Follow-up results on memory tests such as WRBench (2026-06) and [WorldMemBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19063) (2026-10) |
| World models can serve as policy evaluators with a reported correlation to real outcomes: a Veo-based evaluator at Pearson 0.86 for its strongest policy across several out-of-distribution axes ([Google DeepMind, 2025-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10675)) | World models as the policy itself: DreamZero (2026-02), Cosmos Policy (2026-01), 1X (2026-01), all tested by their own authors | As of 2026-10: whether driving simulators (GAIA-3/4, Waymo) publish correlations with real outcomes |
| Visual realism is not physical correctness, nor usefulness to an agent: [Physics-IQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09038) (2025-01), WorldArena (2026-02) | Physics-IQ Verified scores are rising, but the top entry used best-of-N and was vendor-submitted ([leaderboard](https://github.com/google-deepmind/physics-IQ-benchmark), read 2026-10-15) | Like-for-like comparisons; whether outcome distributions are calibrated (Odyssey's [CaliBench](https://odyssey.systems/introducing-calibench), 2026-08, vendor-published) |

"Fairly settled" means a primary result holds in the original paper's setting; it does not mean it holds for other tasks or data.
