# We change. So do our answers.

[中文](perspectives.md) · **English**

One person thinks an offer is too good to turn down. Another thinks it's too far from home. By the end of the conversation, they may both be convinced the other hasn't thought it through.

Often, I think they just need different things. Someone trying to save money, someone wanting a change, and someone with family to care for can look at the same salary and have entirely different decisions to make.

A few years later, my own answer might change too. I might want to live closer to home, or no longer be happy with working hours I once accepted. I don't see much reason to stick to an old plan purely because it's mine.

Admitting that is harder. I really did want it at the time, and I spent a lot of effort getting there. Walking away can feel wasteful. Still, I'd want to ask what I'd choose today if the time already spent weren't part of the decision.

“君子和而不同。” — [*Analects*, 13.23](https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=1428&show=parallel)

Loosely, getting along doesn't require agreeing. I like that. Friends can have a good conversation without arriving at the same decision. Some people love being absorbed in their careers; others want to close the laptop and go home. I'd rather hear why than spend the whole conversation trying to talk them into my preference.

If I'm actually moving or changing jobs, family and partners affected by it need a say, of course. I can't count only the costs that fall on me.

As for how other people live, I'd like to judge a little less when it doesn't hurt anyone. In a few years, I might be asking how they made that choice.
