# Different stages of life can have different answers

[中文](perspectives.md) · **English**

When we ask whether something is “worth it,” we may be asking very different questions. One person is trying to get through the next year. Another is planning for the next ten. Someone else is wondering what they still want to do with their life.

A choice can look quite different depending on the time horizon.

## An earlier answer doesn't have to last a lifetime

Something we wanted badly a few years ago may matter less now. Back then, perhaps we needed security, recognition, or proof that we could do it. Those needs can change while the habit remains: when choosing a path, we still ask which one looks better, pays more, or earns more approval.

I don't think that means the earlier choice was a mistake. Saying “I want something else now” doesn't require dismissing the person who worked so hard to get here.

We might want to prove ourselves after graduation, care more about stability while raising a family, or feel ready to try something different later on. None of that has to happen in a particular order.

## Someone else's answer doesn't have to match mine

Some people genuinely enjoy the satisfaction they get from work; others value their time outside it more. We might be looking at the same decision while facing entirely different responsibilities, expectations, and costs we can afford to bear.

Changing our own priorities doesn't mean people on another path haven't thought theirs through.

**We can find common ground without needing to agree.** Understanding someone's circumstances doesn't mean endorsing every choice they make. We can talk about costs and responsibilities, and still disagree. I'd just rather not treat the answer that fits my current stage of life as a verdict on someone else's whole life.

Leaving a conversation with a better sense of why they chose differently is worthwhile too.

## I'd like to hear your side

Have you come to understand a choice that once made no sense to you, after your circumstances or perspective changed?

You can describe the change in your own thinking without identifying anyone else or sharing private details.
